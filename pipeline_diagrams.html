Radical-class taxonomy 214 Kangxi radicals  ·  engine-implemented (zh_engine.py Step D + configs/zh_radicals.json)

The full Kangxi radical inventory is 214 distinct radicals, the traditional classification system used in every Chinese dictionary since 1716. Each character maps to exactly one radical that encodes a semantic field. ZH gets the full 214 (not collapsed to ~30 for AR symmetry) per per-language-best-potential policy.

Bundle count is bounded by the cross-product of POS × form × tense × voice × mood × num × gender × case × def × clitic stack: 26,256 possible by enumeration, 9,579 validated after running the validator (a 64% rejection by the structural rules of Arabic morphology), 537 attested in 20k sentences. Of those 537 attested, 535 (99.6%) pass the singleton-validator. The original engine-vs-validator gap (697 observed, only 124 passing) was resolved by two engine fixes (VERB demotion when tense or person was unresolvable, def-on-VERB rerouted to NOM, surface-derived num/gender carryover) plus a validator relaxation (NOM-role exemptions for masdars, verbal nouns, plurals, and proper nouns). The residual 2 rejected bundles are closed-class edge cases (the مادام compound and one foreign-script NOM, 3 tokens of 901,068).

wazn-class taxonomy 185 distinct semantic_role values across 348 templates  ·  engine-implemented (ar_engine.py Step B' propagates semantic_role into tags)

The structural asymmetry is the typological prediction the framework now makes explicit: AR is the only language whose information sits on three orthogonal non-concatenative axes (clitic affixation, root identity, wazn semantic role), so it gets four streams. ZH and EN/TR each carry one non-surface signal beyond the baseline tokenizer, so two streams suffice. Vocab caps are tuned per language to fit each language's potential, not for cross-language symmetry.
