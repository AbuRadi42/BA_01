Basque packs three agreement slots into a single auxiliary verb. Subject, object, and indirect object converge like three lanes merging into one tunnel.

Why Lookup Beats Stripping

For most languages, the engine strips suffixes algorithmically. For Basque auxiliaries, this fails because the morphemes are fused beyond separation.

input dut — can you strip this?
try strip d- = ABS.3SG prefix? Maybe... -t = ERG.1SG suffix? Maybe...
problem But "u" is not a separable morpheme — it is fused with the root
verdict Stripping requires knowing all prefix+root+suffix combos → impractical
lookup O(1) hash lookup → 100% accurate, no ambiguity

The -ak Ambiguity

The suffix -ak has two incompatible interpretations. The engine tags both at word level and lets the sentence-level validator decide.

gizonak

gizon + -ak

Interpretation 1
ABS.DEF.PL
"the men" (subject of intransitive OR object)
Interpretation 2
ERG.DEF.SG
"the man" (subject of transitive verb)

At word level: tag BOTH interpretations

At sentence level: validator checks verb auxiliary for ERG/ABS agreement to disambiguate

Ergativity: Case Assignment Algorithm

Unlike most European languages, Basque assigns cases based on verb transitivity. The engine cannot determine this at word level but validates at sentence level.

Is the verb transitive?
YES
Subject → ERG (-k)
Object → ABS (-∅/-a)
NO
Subject → ABS (-∅/-a)

Why This Matters for AI

Basque’s fusional auxiliary verbs are opaque to BPE — subword splits destroy the tri-personal agreement structure. Morph-aware lookup recovers everything.

BPE Tokenization

irakurraraziko diet

irak urrar aziko diet

4 chunks. The root “irakur” is split across two tokens. The causative “-arazi-” is mangled. The auxiliary “diet” is a single token but opaque — its 3 agreement values are invisible.

4 tokens, 0 features exposed
