The Suffix Ambiguity Problem

Why two passes are needed: several Turkish suffixes are identical in form but belong to completely different slots. Without disambiguation, parsing fails.

Suffix Interpretation A Slot Interpretation B Slot
-da / -de ALSO (additive) 13 LOC (locative case) 4
-a / -e OPT (optative mood) 8 DAT (dative case) 4
-an / -en ACT (verbal noun) 10 ABL (ablative, partial) 4

Conflict: Pass 1 assigns to verbal slots, Pass 2 assigns to nominal slots. The selection logic resolves which reading is correct based on context and stem plausibility.

Vowel Harmony Validation

After finding a candidate suffix match, the engine validates it against Turkish vowel harmony rules before accepting.

candidate Candidate suffix found?
2-way check
Stem last vowel {a,ı,o,u} = BACK? → suffix vowel must be BACK
Stem last vowel {e,i,ö,ü} = FRONT? → suffix vowel must be FRONT
4-way check
Stem last vowel rounded (o,ö,u,ü)? → suffix high vowel = rounded
Stem last vowel unrounded (a,e,ı,i)? → suffix high vowel = unrounded
decision
Harmony OK? → Accept match
Harmony fails? → Reject, try next candidate

Stem Plausibility Guard

After stripping a suffix, _stem_plausible() validates that what remains could actually be a Turkish word.

1 Length ≥ 2? (≥ 3 for VOICE/PERSON slots)
2 Contains at least 1 vowel?
✓ Both YES → plausible
✗ Any NO → reject this strip, try next candidate

Why This Matters for AI

Turkish is where morph-aware tokenization delivers its most dramatic gains. The mini experiment proved it.

BPE Tokenization

yazılmayacakmış
yaz ıl may acak mış

5 tokens. BPE splits -ma (NEG) and merges the buffer -y- with the wrong morpheme. Grammatical boundaries are destroyed.

Morph-Aware Tokenization

yazılmayacakmış
yaz -ıl -ma -yacak -mış

5 tokens — but each maps to exactly one morpheme: root + PASS + NEG + FUT + NARR.

56.9%

Measured alignment improvement for Turkish

Mini experiment: 6 models × 3 languages

In the mini experiment, morph-aware tokenization improved Turkish alignment by 56.9% over BPE — the largest gain of any language tested. Turkish’s pure agglutinative structure means every suffix boundary is a meaningful split point that BPE systematically misses. This is not a prediction: it is measured data.
