Decision Tree

How -ing Stripping Works

Word ends in "-ing" AND len > 5? click to expand / collapse

YES → Remove "-ing" → stem

Last 2 chars of stem are same consonant?

e.g., "running" → "runn"

YES → Undouble: "runn" → "run"

Stem ends in consonant (not vowel)?

e.g., "making" → "mak"

YES → Restore silent-e: "mak" → "make"

Otherwise → stem is root

e.g., "playing" → "play"

NO → Skip

Word is too short or doesn't end in -ing. Move to next rule.

Guard Logic

Why "ring", "king", "thing" Are NOT Stripped

The len > 5 guard catches them. Words with 5 or fewer characters that end in "-ing" skip the rule entirely.

ring
✗ len=4
king
✗ len=4
thing
✗ len=5
running
✓ len=7
making
✓ len=6
playing
✓ len=7

The length guard is simple but effective: it prevents the engine from destroying short root words that happen to end in -ing. Similar guards protect -ed (len>4), -s (len>3, not -ss), -es (len>4), and -est (len>5).

Tokenization Impact

Why This Matters for AI

English is already close to BPE’s sweet spot. Morph-aware tokenization yields only minor savings.

Morph-Aware Tokenization

Expected efficiency gain from morph-aware tokenization

English is the low-end baseline. Its shallow morphology leaves little room for compression.
