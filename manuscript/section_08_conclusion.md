# 8. Conclusion and Research Programme

**Status:** Manuscript-prose draft v1. Target length ≈ 500 words.

## 8.1 What this paper establishes

This paper has proposed a framework in which the grammatical information content of a language's word forms — quantified by the Shannon entropy H(L) over feature bundles and the structural recoverability coefficient ρ(L) — predicts the parameter rebate that morphology-aware tokenization delivers over a byte-pair baseline. The framework's inputs are computable from a morphological grammar engine before any training run. Its output, equation (4.7), is a quantitative prediction of how much of a transformer's parameter budget is rebated when grammatical structure is made explicit at the input rather than learned distributionally.

Tested against a six-model Phase 1 experiment spanning English, Arabic, and Turkish — three languages occupying analytic, templatic, and agglutinative positions on the morphological typology — the framework's ordinal prediction held: the observed rebate is monotonically ordered by ρ(L) · H(L), a structural quantity derivable from the grammar alone. The framework's magnitude prediction did not hold: under any reasonable parameterisation of the Hoffmann scaling-law derivative, the implied per-bit coefficient α varies by approximately forty-fold across the three languages. We named this empirical pattern the **Agglutinative Compounding Effect** and argued that two mutually-exclusive interpretations are consistent with Phase 1 evidence: that the true rebate is genuinely super-linear in ρ · H (Interpretation A), or that the standard scaling-law machinery applies only approximately in the Chinchilla-suboptimal regime Phase 1 occupied (Interpretation B).

The three contributions the paper claims are therefore the framework itself; the ordinal empirical result; and the named phenomenon the framework did not anticipate.

## 8.2 Research programme

Two experiments would discriminate between Interpretation A and Interpretation B, and between either of them and the possibility that the framework is wrong in some third way not yet articulated. Both experiments are within reach of the project's existing infrastructure.

**Spectrum expansion at the current scale.** Six additional grammar engines — for German (fusional), Spanish (regular fusional), Hungarian (agglutinative-fusional), Mandarin (isolating), Swahili (classificatory), and Basque (polypersonal, ergative, isolate) — are already built and tested in the project repository. Running the same Phase 1 mini-experiment pipeline across these six engines would produce nine data points spanning the full morphological spectrum plus three structural outliers. Under Interpretation A, the super-linear shape observed in the three-language case should extend smoothly. Under Interpretation B, the language-dependent distortion from Chinchilla-suboptimal training should persist across the expanded set but without a typologically-meaningful pattern. Under a framework-failure reading, the ordinal match of §6.2 should degrade.

**Scale ladder within the existing three languages.** Training each of the six current models at small (20 × 10^6 parameters, 10^8 tokens) and medium (6 × 10^7 parameters, 1.2 × 10^9 tokens) scales would place the loss-to-parameter derivative in a well-characterised Chinchilla-optimal regime. Under Interpretation A, the super-linearity should persist across scales. Under Interpretation B, it should vanish or substantially compress as training approaches optimality.

Together the two experiments produce a 2 × 2 evidence grid against which both interpretations are falsifiable. Neither experiment is conceptually novel; both are bounded in compute cost; both are specified by the framework at the level of concrete engines to be run and metrics to be compared.

**Low-resource edge-deployment showcase for templatic languages.** Arabic's root-based lexicon is vocabulary-efficient — approximately 10,000 triconsonantal roots generate more than 100,000 distinct surface word forms. A root+pattern tokenizer can cover Arabic substantially with a vocabulary an order of magnitude smaller than a conventional BPE tokenizer of equivalent coverage. A targeted extension would train a small (~2 × 10^6 parameter) Arabic model with a radically capped vocabulary under the 1st-tier morph regime of §6 and compare its downstream-task performance (part-of-speech tagging, dialect identification, natural-language inference) against a BPE baseline of matched parameter count and vocabulary budget. A win by the morph model at small vocabulary budget would be a direct demonstration of the framework's edge-hostability claim instantiated for templatic languages, and a concrete equity argument for morphology-aware tokenizers in the low-resource Arabic setting.

**3rd-tier wazn-semantic-class extension for Arabic.** The 2nd-tier experiment of §6.8 exposes root identity as an explicit input stream, but does not expose the semantic function of each wazn — agent noun, patient noun, instrument noun, place/time noun, masdar, sifa mushabbaha, mubalaghah, Form-II causative, Form-X seeking, and so on. These are first-class semantic classes deterministically encoded by the pattern for a native reader, but the current engine flattens them into coarse templatic labels. A 3rd-tier extension would expand the Arabic engine's template inventory into the full semantic-class taxonomy (approximately a day of linguistic-ontology work) and add a fourth input stream wazn_semantic_emb[class] alongside the existing surface-bundle, feature-bundle, and root streams. The marginal Δℒ between 2nd-tier and 3rd-tier Arabic would quantify the wazn-semantic axis of the Templatic Lexical Surplus — strictly additional to the root-identity component measured here, and plausibly comparable in magnitude given how densely Arabic encodes semantic role in the pattern itself. The result would be a three-way decomposition of Arabic's total templatic rebate: surface-recoverable (1st tier), root-identity (2nd tier), wazn-semantic-function (3rd tier).

**Per-model loss attribution on the Arabic compositional bucket.** §6 constructed a 4,747-instance HELD_OUT bucket on the Arabic test corpus consisting of word forms whose (root, template) combination is unseen in training, while both the root and the template are attested separately. A clean per-model evaluation requires assigning per-token loss back to source-word buckets and aggregating only over positions whose source word lies in each bucket. This requires word-to-token alignment maintained separately for the BPE tokeniser, the shallow morph tokeniser, and the engine-root tokeniser. With that infrastructure in place, the test would compare Δℒ(morph − baseline) on HELD_OUT against the same on SEEN_PAIR; a larger morph advantage on HELD_OUT would be direct evidence that morph-aware tokenisation induces compositional learning of Arabic's root–template system, rather than memorisation of surface-bundle co-occurrences.

**Cross-Semitic transfer of the Arabic engine structure.** Arabic's root-pattern morphology is a shared structural property of the Semitic language family — Hebrew, Aramaic, Amharic, Maltese, and others. The Arabic grammar engine of §5.2 is in principle portable to related languages with modest changes to the root extractor and the template inventory. A lighter-weight follow-up would port the engine to Hebrew or Maltese and re-compute H(L), ρ(L), and ρ · H without training any models, demonstrating that the framework's pre-training predictive signal extends to a Semitic family with negligible additional infrastructure. This reach is important: the framework's practical case is strongest for languages that are morphologically rich and digital-resource-poor, and the Semitic family contains several members in exactly that position.

## 8.3 Stance

The paper's position is that a well-formed theoretical framework that orders three typologically distant languages correctly, while failing to predict their magnitudes under any current scaling law, is more scientifically productive than either a confirmatory gradient result or a scaling-law extension with no structural grounding. The Agglutinative Compounding Effect — whether it survives as a real property of morphology-aware language modelling or dissolves into a correction term on existing scaling laws — is the question the framework was built to ask. The research programme of §8.2 is what asking the question completely would entail.
